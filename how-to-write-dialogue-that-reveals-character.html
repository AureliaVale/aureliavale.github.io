How to Write Dialogue That Reveals Character

Amateur dialogue tells you what characters are thinking. Professional dialogue shows you who they are.

The best dialogue works on at least two levels simultaneously: what is being said, and what is being avoided. A scene where two characters discuss the weather while both are thinking about their dying marriage is not a scene about weather. The weather is the surface. What they cannot say is the scene.

Every character should have a distinct voice — not just a different vocabulary, but a different relationship to language. One character might speak in long, careful sentences. Another in fragments. A third might use humour as a reflex, deflecting anything emotionally direct.

Dialogue also has rhythm. Read it aloud. If it sounds like prose with quotation marks around it, it is not working.
