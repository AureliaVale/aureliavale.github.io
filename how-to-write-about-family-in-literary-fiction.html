How to Write About Family in Literary Fiction

Family is the oldest subject of literary fiction, and the most inexhaustible. Because no relationship is as formative, as complicated, or as difficult to see clearly from the inside.

The challenge of writing family is not finding the material — everyone has it. The challenge is finding the distance to write it truthfully. Too close, and the writing becomes complaint or tribute, unable to see the people involved as the contradictory, fallible, deeply human figures they are. Too far, and the emotional truth drains away.

The best fiction about family is specific. Not "a difficult mother" but this mother, with this particular combination of love and damage, in this particular house, in this particular year. Specificity is what transforms the personal into the universal — paradoxically, the more particular a family portrait is, the more readers will recognise themselves in it.

The Deeper Story was written for writers who want to work at this depth.
