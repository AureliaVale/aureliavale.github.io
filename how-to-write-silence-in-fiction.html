How to Write Silence in Fiction

Some of the most powerful moments in literary fiction contain no words at all.

Hemingway understood this. His iceberg theory — that the dignity of movement of an iceberg is due to only one-eighth of it being above water — was a theory about silence as much as brevity.

Silence in fiction operates on several levels. There is the silence between characters — the conversation that does not happen, the words that are swallowed. There is the silence within a character — the thought they do not let themselves finish. And there is the silence of the narrative itself — the gap between scenes, the years summarised in a sentence.

The technique is to make the silence visible without filling it. To let the reader feel the shape of what is absent. This can be done through behaviour — a character who changes the subject every time a particular name comes up. Through environment — a room that is never entered. Through syntax — a sentence that stops before it should.

Silence, handled well, is not emptiness. It is the fullest thing on the page.

The Deeper Story was written for writers who want to work at this depth.
