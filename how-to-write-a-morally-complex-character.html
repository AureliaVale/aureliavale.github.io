How to Write a Character Who Is Morally Complex

The most memorable characters in literary fiction are rarely wholly good or wholly bad. They are something more uncomfortable: recognisably human.

Moral complexity means that a character's inner life contains contradictions that cannot be easily resolved. They want things that conflict with each other. They hold values they do not always live up to. They understand, sometimes, the harm they cause — and proceed anyway.

The first step is to understand what your character wants, and what that wanting costs others. The second is to resist the urge to judge your character from outside the story. The narrator's job is not to tell the reader how to feel — it is to render the character so completely that the reader feels the full weight of them.

The third step is to give your character a moment of genuine choice — not between good and evil, but between two goods, or two evils, or a good that costs something real. That choice defines them.

The Deeper Story was written for writers who want to work at this depth.
