How to Write Place in Literary Fiction

In literary fiction, place is never merely a setting. It is a character — with history, with atmosphere, with the capacity to act upon the people who inhabit it.

The writers who use place most powerfully are those who understand that landscape and cityscape carry meaning — that a specific street in a specific city at a specific hour is not interchangeable with any other, and that precision in rendering place creates the kind of reality that makes a reader feel they are there rather than reading about there.

But place in literary fiction is also always filtered through consciousness. The same street looks different to a woman leaving a funeral and a teenager in love. What a character notices about a place reveals who they are. What they fail to notice reveals it even more.

Write place from the inside of a particular subjectivity. Let the place be real, and let it be seen.
