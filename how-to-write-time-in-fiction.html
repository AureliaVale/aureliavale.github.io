How to Write Time in Fiction

Time in fiction is not a clock. It is a lens.

A novel can spend fifty pages on a single afternoon and two sentences on a decade. What determines how time moves in fiction is not chronology but significance — the weight the narrative assigns to each moment.

The dangerous assumption is that time should move steadily, scene by scene, at roughly the same pace. This produces fiction that feels dutiful but airless. The reader moves through it without surprise, without the sensation of being inside a life rather than a summary of one.

Learn to modulate. Let the narrative linger where the story needs it — inside the charged moment, the conversation that changes everything. Let it accelerate through the stretches of time that are necessary but not alive. And mark the transitions clearly enough that the reader is never disoriented, only moved.

The Deeper Story was written for writers who want to work at this depth.
