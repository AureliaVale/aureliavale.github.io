How to Read Like a Writer

Reading like a writer is a different activity from reading like a reader. A reader asks: what happens next? A writer asks: how is this done?

This means slowing down. Stopping at the passages that affect you most strongly and asking not just what they made you feel, but how they made you feel it. What specific choices — of word, of sentence length, of what is included and what is left out — created that effect?

It also means reading widely and against your preferences. The writers who will teach you the most are not always the ones you enjoy most. Sometimes the writer who does something you dislike is teaching you, precisely through your dislike, what you do not want to do.

Keep a notebook for this. Not for plot summaries or character names. For the sentences that stopped you. For the moments you understood, for the first time, how a particular thing could be done.
