Point of View in Fiction: Choosing the Right Narrator

The choice of point of view is one of the most consequential decisions a fiction writer makes — and it is often made too quickly.

First person narration creates intimacy and immediacy. The reader is inside a single consciousness, limited by it, close to it. The unreliable narrator is native to this mode.

Third person limited maintains slight distance while retaining intimacy — a slightly wider view without losing closeness to one consciousness.

Third person omniscient allows breadth but risks losing the intimacy contemporary readers often expect.

The question is not "which point of view is best" but "which serves this particular story?" A story about self-deception belongs in first person. A story about the gap between how we see ourselves and how others see us may need third. The answer is different every time.
