How to Write a Scene That Stays With the Reader

Some scenes, once read, cannot be forgotten. They become part of a reader's interior life — recalled years later, returning unbidden.

The first element is specificity. The specific detail — the particular colour of a dress, the sound a door makes, the exact words spoken — gives the mind something to grip. Vague scenes leave nothing to hold.

The second element is emotional truth. Readers sense the difference between a writer who has understood something and one who is describing what understanding looks like from the outside.

The third element is consequence. A scene that stays is one where something changes — where the world of the story is different after it than before. And the fourth is restraint. The most powerful scenes are those where the writer trusts the reader.

The Deeper Story was written for writers who want to work at this depth.
